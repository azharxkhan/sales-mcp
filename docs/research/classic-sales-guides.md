# Classic Sales Guides: 1887 to the 2000s

> What salespeople read before the internet and AI, what each guide taught, and what it still says about AI-era selling.
> Researched 2026-11-11. Rows marked *(bg)* rely on general background knowledge and were not re-verified in this pass. Check the dates before quoting them.
> **No files are stored in this repo.** Public-domain titles are linked to Project Gutenberg / Internet Archive. Copyrighted books should be bought or borrowed.

---

## 1. Timeline

### The scripted era (1880s–1920s): selling as a repeatable process
| Year | Guide | Author | Core idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1887 | **The NCR Primer** ("How I Sell National Cash Registers") | Joseph H. Crane, made mandatory by John H. Patterson (NCR) | The first widely used sales script: **approach → proposition → demonstration → close**. Agents had to memorise ~450 words, and those who didn't were fired. NCR then opened a formal sales school in Dayton (c. 1894) |
| 1898 | **AIDA** model | E. St. Elmo Lewis | Attention → Interest → Desire → Action. Still the backbone of sales and ad copy |
| c. 1900s–10s | Sheldon School of Salesmanship (correspondence courses) | Arthur F. Sheldon *(bg)* | Selling as a learnable "science". Character and service as the basis of sales |
| 1912 | **[The Psychology of Salesmanship](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/41510)** | William Walker Atkinson | Understanding the buyer's mind. Public domain (US) |
| 1910s | **[Ginger Talks](https://archive.org/search?query=Ginger+Talks+Holman)** (Internet Archive search) | Worthington C. Holman | Pep talks for salesmen, the start of the motivational genre |
| c. 1918–1920 | **[The Selling Process](https://archive.org/download/sellingprocessha00hawkrich/sellingprocessha00hawkrich_bw.pdf)** / **[Certain Success](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=Certain+Success+Hawkins)** | Norval A. Hawkins (Ford's sales manager) | A systematic, step-by-step selling process from a manager who scaled Ford's dealer network *(bg for the Ford role)* |
| 1920s | **[Letters From an Old Time Salesman to His Son](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/64277)** | Roy Lester James | Practical advice, informally written. Public domain (US) |
| 1920s | **[Selling Things](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/59176)** | Orison Swett Marden & J. F. MacGrail | Personality, persistence and service. Public domain (US) |
| 1925 | *The Psychology of Selling and Advertising* | E. K. Strong *(bg)* | Academic treatment that popularised AIDA |

### The relationship and persuasion era (1930s–1960s)
| Year | Guide | Author | Core idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1936 | *How to Win Friends and Influence People* | Dale Carnegie | Genuine interest, use people's names, make them feel important |
| 1937 | *Tested Sentences That Sell* | Elmer Wheeler *(bg)* | "Don't sell the steak, sell the sizzle." Wheeler **tested phrases on real customers**, an early form of A/B testing |
| 1947 | *How I Raised Myself from Failure to Success in Selling* | Frank Bettger | Enthusiasm, record-keeping, and finding out what the customer wants, then helping them get it |
| 1968 | *The Greatest Salesman in the World* | Og Mandino | A motivational parable about habits and persistence |

### The professional / consultative era (1970s–1990s)
| Year | Guide | Author | Core idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1970 | *Consultative Selling* | Mack Hanan *(bg)* | Sell improved profit, acting as the customer's consultant |
| 1977 | *How to Sell Anything to Anybody* | Joe Girard | **"Girard's Law of 250":** every customer influences about 250 others, so one bad experience spreads. Relentless follow-up and personal notes |
| 1980 | *How to Master the Art of Selling* | Tom Hopkins | Practical techniques for every stage, from prospecting to closing |
| 1984 | *Secrets of Closing the Sale* | Zig Ziglar | ~100 closing techniques, and the idea that selling is helping |
| 1985 | *Strategic Selling* | Robert Miller & Stephen Heiman | Complex sales with multiple buyer roles (economic, user, technical buyers, coach). Account planning |
| 1985 | *The Psychology of Selling* (audio programme; book 2004) | Brian Tracy *(bg)* | Self-concept, goal setting, the inner game |
| 1988 | **SPIN Selling** | Neil Rackham | 35,000 calls studied over 12 years. **Closing techniques that work in small sales *hurt* large ones.** Discovery questions win big deals |
| 1994 | *The Sales Bible* | Jeffrey Gitomer | Practical, contrarian rules: "people don't like to be sold, but they love to buy" |
| 1995 | *Solution Selling* | Michael Bosworth | Diagnose pain before prescribing. Selling to the buyer's vision |
| 1995 | *You Can't Teach a Kid to Ride a Bike at a Seminar* | David Sandler | The **Sandler system** (created in 1967): up-front contracts, a no-pressure "mutual agreement", qualifying out early |
| 1999 | *Rethinking the Sales Force* | Neil Rackham & John DeVincentis | Transactional vs consultative vs enterprise selling. Not every customer wants a rep |

### Early 2000s: bridge to the modern era
| Year | Guide | Author | Core idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2002 | *Hope Is Not a Strategy* | Rick Page *(bg)* | Complex-sale process discipline |
| 2003 | *CustomerCentric Selling* | Michael Bosworth & John Holland *(bg)* | Conversations instead of presentations. Sales-ready messaging |
| 2004 | *The Little Red Book of Selling* | Jeffrey Gitomer | Value-first, relationship-driven selling |
| 2005 | *Selling to Big Companies* | Jill Konrath *(bg)* | Getting time with busy corporate buyers |
| 2006 | *Predictable Revenue* (published 2011, practice from ~2003) | Aaron Ross | The specialised SDR model at Salesforce, the direct ancestor of AI SDRs |
| 2007 | *The Ultimate Sales Machine* | Chet Holmes *(bg)* | Focused, repeatable sales systems |

## 2. What the classics already knew about AI-era problems

| AI-era complaint | What the classics said | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Bots break when you go off script** | NCR's Primer had the same problem: scripted reps struggled outside the script. NCR fixed it with **training, objection handling and judgement**, not longer scripts | NCR Primer and sales school |
| **Generic mass outreach** | Carnegie: use their name and be genuinely interested. Girard: one bad experience reaches ~250 people. Mass AI outreach multiplies the bad experiences | Carnegie 1936, Girard 1977 |
| **Pushy AI closing tactics** | Rackham's research: high-pressure closing **lowers** success in large B2B sales. Questions and discovery win | SPIN 1988 |
| **Hidden selling and manipulation** | Gitomer: people hate being sold to but love to buy. Sandler: up-front, no-pressure agreements | Gitomer 1994, Sandler |
| **"Template" messaging** | Wheeler *tested* every sentence on real customers before scaling it. Templates were fine, but only after testing. AI blasts skipped that step | Wheeler 1937 |
| **Not every buyer wants a rep** | Rackham & DeVincentis predicted it in 1999: transactional buyers want efficiency (→ self-service and AI), and consultative buyers want expertise (→ humans) | *Rethinking the Sales Force* 1999 |
| **Replacing people** | Bettger and Ziglar: selling is helping someone get what they want, which is a human service. Tools should serve that | Bettger 1947, Ziglar 1984 |

**Bottom line:** the AI failures of 2024–25 repeated old mistakes (scripts without judgement, volume without relevance, pressure without discovery) at machine scale. The fixes the classics found (discovery, testing, follow-up with care, matching the channel to the buyer) are the ones that work for AI too.

## 3. Using these in the Sales MCP

- **AIDA / NCR's four steps** → the `/quote-for-customer` prompt follows a structured flow without pressure.
- **SPIN** → the `/needs-assessment` prompt asks Situation, Problem, Implication and Need-payoff questions before quoting.
- **Strategic Selling / MEDDIC** → the Deal entity tracks buyer roles and qualification (M7).
- **Rackham & DeVincentis' segmentation** → the MCP serves transactional buyers (self-service), and `request_human_review` routes consultative needs to people.
- **Girard's follow-up** → event-driven follow-up prompts, not blasts.
